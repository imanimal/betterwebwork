How to type answers in WeBWorK

WeBWorK reads answers as typed text, so a right answer written the wrong way is marked incorrect. This page covers how to write each kind of answer, the slips that cost the most attempts, and what WeBWorK's error messages mean.

Two habits save the most attempts: press Preview My Answers before you submit, since it shows how WeBWorK read your answer and doesn't use an attempt; and when a problem's own instructions say something different from this page, follow the problem.

Problem 2

Functions

Always put a function's input in parentheses: sin(x), ln(x+1), sqrt(2). Without them WeBWorK may take only part of what follows as the input, or read the letters as variables multiplied together.

Problem 4

Why a right answer gets marked wrong

Rounding too early

WeBWorK usually accepts a number within 0.1% of the right one. 0.33 for 1/3 is 1% off and is marked wrong. Give at least four significant digits (0.3333), or the exact value (1/3). Keep full precision in the middle steps too, not only in the final answer.

An ambiguous fraction

1/2x can be read as one half of x or as one over 2x. Write (1/2)x or 1/(2x) so there's only one reading, and preview it.

Symbols copied from somewhere else

A ×, ÷, − (a long minus), √, π, ² or ° pasted from notes or a calculator often can't be read. Type them as *, /, -, sqrt( ), pi and ^2, and leave out the degree sign.

The wrong variable

Variables are case-sensitive: X and x are different. Use the letters the problem uses, and t if the problem is in terms of t.

A decimal where an exact answer is wanted

Some problems want an exact answer: 2/3, sqrt(2), pi/4. A decimal, however precise, isn't accepted there.

Part of it is right

Lists, intervals and multi-part blanks give credit for each correct piece. Keep the pieces you're sure of and change the rest.

Problem 5

WeBWorK's error messages, explained

WeBWorK's messages come from its answer checker and can be terse. What they usually mean:

Unexpected character '…'

WeBWorK found a symbol it can't read, often one pasted in (×, −, π, √, ²). Type it with the keys on your keyboard instead.

Variable 'X' is not defined in this context

The answer uses a letter the problem doesn't. Check the case (X versus x). If the name is longer, like 'sni' or 'xy', it's a misspelled function or letters multiplied without *.

Your answer isn't a number (it looks like a formula that returns a number)

The blank wants one value, but your answer still has a variable in it. Work it out to a single number.

Your answer isn't a formula that returns a number (it looks like a number)

The opposite: the blank wants an expression in the variable, and your answer is a plain number.

Your answer isn't a number (it looks like a list of numbers)

A comma turned your answer into a list. Leave out commas, including in numbers like 1,000.

Missing operand after '*'

An operator is missing a value on one side, as in 2* or *x. Often a typo or a deleted term.

Missing or extra parentheses

Count them: every ( needs a ). Intervals mix brackets on purpose, [2, 5), and that's fine.

Function 'sqrt' has too many inputs

A comma or a misplaced parenthesis inside the function, like sqrt(2,3).

Can't generate enough valid points for comparison

WeBWorK checks a formula by plugging numbers into yours and the right one. Yours couldn't be worked out at enough of them: a square root or log of a negative number, or a division by zero. Check the domain of each piece.

Division by zero

Your expression divides by zero at a value WeBWorK tried. Check denominators, and whether a factor cancels to 0.

Function 'sin' is not allowed in this context

The problem doesn't let you use that function or operator here, usually so you work the value out yourself. Simplify it away.
